Choose your unit of success

The unit is what you count when the outcome happens. It isn't always "people helped."

BusinessesConsultant: leadership acts on an evidence-based decision
LearnersEducator: applies a new skill at work
HouseholdsFinancial coach: adopts and uses a financial plan
GuestsHospitality: leaves feeling restored or welcomed
ProjectsCreative studio: a client's project is completed
AcresEnvironmental nonprofit: land restored to habitat
ConnectionsCommunity group: members form lasting relationships

For abstract or experiential missions, three tests help:

  1. What changes? Name the person, group, place or thing that is different afterward.
  2. Would you recognize one? If you can't tell when a single instance has happened, the unit is still too vague.
  3. Can it be counted honestly? Prefer a unit you can evidence over one that only sounds meaningful.
Stage 4  ·  Explore the Metric Menu

How could you see that change?

A metric you can't sense is a metric you can't learn from.

Four levels of evidence

Every metric carries a level. The level tells you what the number can and cannot prove. Call a metric what it is: a book sale is reach, and attendance is engagement at most.

Reach

Your contribution reached someone. Sales, downloads, attendance.

Doesn't prove anyone read, used or changed.

Engagement

They actively took part. Completions, questions, follow-up bookings.

Doesn't prove anything changed.

Behavior change

They did something differently. Follow-up check-ins, observed adoption.

Doesn't prove it led to the result.

Realized impact

The change you exist for happened. Milestones, decisions implemented, acres restored.

Doesn't prove you were the only cause.
